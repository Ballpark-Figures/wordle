# Deep families — what hard mode has to walk one word at a time

The families of answers that force hard mode past six guesses, and the selection
kept for the video.

Generated by `bash solver/run-deep-families.sh`; `math/tools/deep_families.py`
states the derivation and IS the source of truth. Exact and deterministic — a
pure function of the committed `data/shared/hardturns/trees`, no sampling and no
seed, so a re-run reproduces the CSV byte for byte (~16s).

## The mechanism

Hard mode requires every green to be repeated in place. So an opener that pins a
skeleton **without locating the varying letter** leaves the legal set EQUAL to a
family of answers differing in one position — and the solver has no option but to
name the members one at a time.

The deepest line in the whole hard-mode sweep:

    ought  eight  fight  light  might  night  right  sight  tight  wight

Ten guesses, nine of them spent walking `.ight` in alphabetical order. `wight` is
the deepest answer in hard mode at depth 10, and it is deep for one reason: the
family holds nine words and `w` sorts last. Normal mode would probe with
something like `wharf` and separate all nine at once; hard mode forbids it.

**This is a hard-mode-only phenomenon.** Normal mode caps every answer at 6 by
construction (`data/shared/optturns/opener_totals.csv` has zero INFEASIBLE rows),
so the same scan there returns nothing.

## Reading the columns, and the one trap

`size` counts answers matching the template. `walk` counts the guesses actually
spent inside it. **They can differ a lot, and when they do the row is not a
family in the sense the tight ones are** — `...er` matches 141 answers and its
walk was seven words of seven different shapes (`twier sorer layer under cheer
ember fever`). That is hard mode pinning `-er` and there simply being too many
candidates, not a family being walked. `--min-fixed` excludes those, and the
video selection's "at least 3 fixed letters" rule is doing exactly that job.

`openers` is how many of the 12972 openers reach that depth on that answer, and
it does NOT track depth. `.illy` is only depth 7 but **275 openers** land in it —
by far the most common trap — while the depth-10 `.ight` catches only 14. So the
deepest families are not the ones a player is likely to meet; the
expensive-in-practice ones are the shallow, high-traffic `.illy` / `.atty` /
`.atch` group.

`kin` counts the shallower members of the same family that were folded away, so
nothing is lost by keeping only the deepest. `.ight` and `.ound` fold away six
each.

## Two derivation choices that matter

**The family is TIGHTEST, not longest.** Among trailing runs of two or more
guesses, the one fixing the MOST positions wins. Taking the longest run drags the
OPENER in — the word that by definition did not match — which both mislabels the
family (`..ght` instead of `.ight`, since `ought` has `o` where the other nine
have `i`) and SPLITS it, putting `wight` at depth 10 and `right` at depth 8 in
different groups though they are the same trap.

**Grouping is by the matched answer SET, not the template string.** `.ight` and
`.i.ht` are different strings matching the identical nine answers, so they are
one family, and the reported name is re-derived from that set.
